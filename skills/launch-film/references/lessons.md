# Lessons from real launch films

What was approved and what was rejected across several rounds of launch films built with this workflow. Each line cost at least 1 round. Newer lessons win. Add your own verdicts at the bottom in the owner's words, so the next film starts smarter.

## Direction

| Approved | Rejected |
|---|---|
| Apple and Lovable grammar: supreme motion graphics with room to breathe, clean type, fast pace, on brand | Slow title cards; they read dated |
| Story over feature tours: less about features, more about the story, short | A tour of 5 panels with mood shots in between; it read as AI filler |
| 1 shared body template behind every hook | Building a new product walkthrough per film |
| Detail and clarity: name the features so the viewer can follow | Mood without the product |
| Hero words cut in hard, much more dynamic | An icon with coloured text in a fixed slot, beat after beat; it read monotone |

## Look

| Approved | Rejected |
|---|---|
| A warm light ground with a soft bloom of the brand colour | Strong brand colour on long runs of type; a neon or lime cast |
| The logo in its plain, calm form | A logo recoloured into a loud brand colour for the film |
| Glass on a dark brand ground with light drifting behind it | Strong white cards on flat grey |
| A 3D object when depth carries meaning (a graph, a system) | A flat, barely visible diagram |
| Transitions that mean something (characters building an object) | Textures with no meaning (a cutting mat, a dither spiral): "random" |
| A clean end: the lockup, then quiet | A busy end card |
| Warm looks, slightly lifted | Pure black and white plates with crushed blacks |

## Timing and voice

| Approved | Rejected |
|---|---|
| A brisk, clear narrator | Lines with gaps; badly spoken, too slow |
| Type on the beat of the voice | Key words landing 0.1 to 0.3 s late; viewers call it "off beat" |
| 1 voice through the film | A second voice for a new line, even a clear one |
| A snappy, punchy cut into the brand | A slow transition into the tagline |
| Short copy that says the result | Weak generic lines ("everything gets tracked"); text cards the animation already says |

## Footage and rights

| Approved | Rejected |
|---|---|
| Generated, fictional people in a calm, well-dressed set | Famous founders or any real person to advertise a product |
| Realistic period video when realism is the point | Black and white archival for a modern story; stills with fake camera moves |
| A stylised 3D animated character when photoreal looks fake | Photoreal people that look fake |
| Generated footage improved with better prompts and anchors | Drawn or vector stand-ins for footage; they read cheap |
| The era and genre of a famous film | The film itself, or its actor |

## Picture craft

- The whole app window at tiny size is out. Push into the part the voice names.
- No monotone stretch longer than a few seconds: zoom in, add colour, change the ground.
- A hero word holds at least about 0.6 s; the end holds quiet for about 4 s.
- Continuity at every cut: the outgoing element is still moving, the incoming one arrives in motion.
- A film that passed every number still read as basic. Lead with the eye; numbers only point at problems.

## Process

- Show frames before building a whole film. A wrong look costs a day; a contact sheet costs minutes.
- Review voice and music inside a full film, never as loose audio clips. Change 1 variable per round.
- Every body change goes to every film (`film/render.sh`).
- State the cost of every paid video job and wait for a yes.
